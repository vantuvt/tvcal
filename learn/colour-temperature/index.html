Why “Warm” is the correct white

Switch a TV to its Warm colour temperature and the picture looks yellow for a minute. Give your eyes time, though, and Warm is almost always the setting closest to the white the film was made with. Cool looks brighter and crisper, but it’s wrong.

What colour temperature is

A TV makes white by mixing red, green and blue light. Tip the mix towards blue and white looks cool; tip it towards red and it looks warm. Colour temperature describes that balance, in kelvin: higher numbers are bluer.

It affects everything on screen, not just white. Skin, grass and sky all take on the same tint, which is why the wrong setting makes faces look pale and blue, or orange.

The white everything is made for

Films, TV shows and streaming video are graded on studio monitors set to one standard white, called D65. It’s roughly 6500 K, like daylight at midday. The aim at home is the same white, so you see the colours the colourist chose.

Most TVs come out of the box in a Standard or Cool setting, well above 6500 K. Next to other TVs in a shop, a bluer white looks brighter and more vivid. At home, it makes everything look slightly cold.

On most TVs, a Warm option is the closest to D65. Exactly which one varies by brand and model, and getting it precisely right needs a measuring instrument. By eye, the warmest option, or one step cooler, gets you most of the way.

Why Warm looks wrong at first

Your eyes adapt to whatever white they’ve been looking at. If your TV has been on Cool for years, the correct white looks yellow by comparison. After a minute or two, it starts to look neutral, and switching back to Cool looks distinctly blue.

That’s why the setup asks you to wait before judging, and to look at the white and grey areas rather than at colourful ones.

Not the same as Colour or Tint

Colour temperature
The colour of white and greys: blue to yellow. Set it to Warm.
Colour (or Saturation)
How strong colours are. Leave it at the picture mode’s default.
Tint (or Hue)
Shifts colours between green and magenta. Leave it at the default.

Filmmaker Mode, where your TV has it, sets the standard white for you, along with switching off most extra processing.

What your TV calls it
