Local dimming and blooming

On an LCD TV, local dimming is what makes black look black. It’s also why subtitles, stars and white text sometimes wear a faint glow on a dark background. Both come from the same idea.

Why LCD blacks glow

An LCD TV has a backlight behind the screen and a layer of liquid crystals that block or let through its light. The crystals can’t block it completely, so some always leaks through. With the backlight fully on, black comes out as a dark grey, most visibly in a dark room.

What local dimming does

Local dimming splits the backlight into zones and turns each one down behind dark parts of the picture. Where the scene is black, the light behind it is too, so very little leaks through.

The zones are much bigger than pixels, though. A zone behind a small bright object, like a star or a line of subtitles, has to light up for it, and it lights the black around it too. That halo is blooming. It’s worse when you sit off to one side.

More zones means smaller halos. Mini-LED TVs use thousands of tiny LEDs and can have hundreds or thousands of zones. Cheaper edge-lit TVs light the screen from its edges and may have only a handful, so dimming shows up as bright columns or bands.

OLED doesn’t need it

Each pixel on an OLED TV makes its own light and switches off completely for black. There’s no backlight, so there’s no glow and no blooming, and OLED TVs have no local dimming setting.

How to set it

Most LCD TVs offer Off, Low, Medium and High:

Higher
Deeper blacks and brighter highlights, but more blooming. On some TVs it also dims small highlights or hides shadow detail.
Lower
Less blooming and truer shadows, but greyer blacks.

Medium is a good place to start. Go higher if blacks look grey in a dark room, lower if halos distract you or dark detail disappears. Avoid Off unless blooming really bothers you: blacks will be noticeably grey.

The setup’s black-level pattern surrounds its boxes with grey frames, so the zones stay lit as they would in a real dark scene. If box B never appears however high you set black level, local dimming is hiding it, and turning it down one step usually fixes it.
