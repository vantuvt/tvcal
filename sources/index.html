Before you start

Set up your streaming box or console

Get the box sending the right signal first. Otherwise you end up adjusting the TV to cover for it, and the settings are wrong for everything else.

Apple TV 4K

Turn on content matching, so the Apple TV switches to each show’s own format instead of sending everything one way.

Match Dynamic RangeSettings → Video and Audio → Match Content
OnSends HDR only for HDR content. Without it, the test patterns may be shown in HDR and look washed out.
Match Frame RateSettings → Video and Audio → Match Content
OnFilms play at their own frame rate, so slow pans don’t stutter.

Make the TV’s picture changes on the HDMI input the Apple TV uses: most TVs keep settings per input.

Chromecast with Google TV and Google TV Streamer

Set it to follow the content. TVs with Google TV built in don’t need any of this.

Dynamic rangeSettings → Display & Sound
Match contentHDR only for HDR shows. Without it, ordinary video and the SDR test patterns can be shown in HDR. On some versions it’s under Advanced display settings.
Match content frame rateSettings → Display & Sound
OnFilms play at their own frame rate.
ResolutionSettings → Display & Sound → Resolution
4K or AutoOn a 4K TV. At 1080p, the TV has to upscale everything you watch.

Amazon Fire TV

Let the stick follow the content rather than forcing one format.

Match Original Frame RateDisplay settings
OnIt’s off by default. Films then play at their own frame rate.
Dynamic rangeDisplay settings
Follow the contentIf you see a choice, pick the option that switches to HDR only for HDR content, not the one that always outputs HDR.

PlayStation 5

Set the output, then run the console’s own HDR adjustment with the TV in its game mode.

RGB RangeSettings → Screen and Video → Video Output
AutomaticConsole and TV agree on where black is. Only change it if the TV doesn’t read it correctly.
HDRSettings → Screen and Video → Video Output
On When SupportedHDR for HDR games and apps; everything else stays SDR. Always On converts SDR to HDR.
Adjust HDRSettings → Screen and Video → Video Output
Follow the on-screen stepsTells games how bright your TV can go.

Xbox Series X|S

Use the standard colour space for a TV, and calibrate HDR for games.

Colour spaceTV & display options
Standard (recommended)Right for TVs. PC RGB is for monitors set to full range.
Allow HDR10Settings → General → TV & display options → Advanced → Video modes
OnLets HDR games and apps use HDR. Auto HDR, which adds HDR to older games, is a personal choice.
Calibrate HDR for gamesSettings → General → TV & display options
Follow the on-screen stepsTells games how bright your TV can go.

Windows PC

PCs are the most common cause of a range mismatch, because graphics cards often send full range while TVs expect limited.

Output rangeGraphics driver display settings
Match the TVFull on the PC with the TV’s HDMI input set to full (often called PC mode), or limited on both. Either works; mixing them doesn’t.
HDRWindows display settings
On for HDR games and filmsThe desktop can look dull in HDR; turn it off for everyday use if you prefer.
Windows HDR CalibrationFree app from Microsoft
Run once with HDR onTells games how bright your TV can go.

Menu names are from each maker’s documentation where available, and can change with software updates:
  • Apple: Turn on Match Dynamic Range or Match Frame Rate
  • Google: Display settings for Chromecast with Google TV and Google TV Streamer
  • PlayStation: PS5 video output settings
  • Xbox Wire: Xbox Series X|S TV settings
  • AFTVnews: Fire TV frame rate matching

The three things to check

Dynamic range. Send HDR only when the show is HDR. Forcing everything into HDR makes ordinary video look dull.

Frame rate. Match the film’s rate, often 24 frames a second, so pans don’t stutter.

Colour range. Box and TV must agree, or blacks turn grey or crush.
