Brand guide

Hisense picture setup

Hisense TVs use either VIDAA or Google TV, and menus differ a little between them and between years. If a name doesn’t match, look for the closest one.

Watching through a streaming box or console? Set that up first: a mismatch there makes blacks grey or crushed whatever you do on the TV.

01

Get ready

  • Update your TV’s software first: Settings → Support → System Update on VIDAA models, or Settings → System → About → System update on Google TV models. Updates can change picture processing or reset settings, so do it before you start.
  • Turn the lights down to how you normally watch.
  • Open the picture menu with the remote: Settings → Picture (VIDAA) or Settings → Display & Sound → Picture (Google TV). You’ll use it throughout.
02

Quick settings

Do these first. They make the biggest difference.

  • Picture Mode: Filmmaker Mode or Theater NightSome models call it Cinema Night. Avoid the Energy Saving mode, which limits the backlight.
  • Motion Enhancement: OffUnder Clarity or Motion settings (Motion Smoothing on some Google TV models). Motion Clearness (sharper motion, but can flicker) is a personal choice; leave it off if unsure.
  • Automatic Light Sensor: OffOften under Picture → General. Also avoid the Energy Saving picture mode.
  • Color Space: AutoUnder Color settings. Avoid Native.
  • Color and Tint: default; Active Contrast OffAlso turn off any other “dynamic” or “enhanced” picture options.
  • Gamma: 2.4 or BT1886 in a dark room, 2.2 in a bright oneUnder Brightness settings.
  • Noise Reduction and MPEG / Digital Noise Reduction: OffUnder Clarity settings.
Where are these settings?

VIDAA: Settings → Picture. Google TV: Settings → Display & Sound → Picture. The detailed controls are grouped as Brightness, Color, Clarity and Motion (some under Advanced Settings).

Hisense keeps separate settings for HDR content, so the HDR steps later will check those.

03

Fit to screen

Look at the edges of your TV. There’s a thin white border around the very edge, with arrows in each corner.

Can you see the white border on all four edges, with arrows touching the corners?

If not: turn Overscan offLook for Overscan in the Picture or Advanced settings (on some models under Picture Size or Aspect Ratio) and set it to Off.

Uses the fit-to-screen test pattern. The guided setup shows it on your TV for you.

04

Brightness

This makes dark scenes look right: true black, with shadow detail still visible.

Most Hisense TVs have both Backlight (overall light output) and Brightness (black level): use Brightness for this step and set Backlight for comfort.

If yours has no Backlight setting, Brightness is the light output instead: look for a Black Level setting for this step.

Look inside the grey frames. When it’s right, box A is completely gone and box B is a very faint grey square.

Not changing? If your Hisense has no separate Backlight setting, Brightness may be changing the light output; look for Black Level instead. If box B never appears, see “About Local Dimming” below.

About Local Dimming

Many LCD TVs dim parts of the backlight in dark areas. On this TV it’s called Local Dimming.

The grey frames around the boxes keep the backlight on, like in a real dark scene. Judge the boxes inside the frames, not the black edges of the screen.

If box B still never appears, even after raising Brightness a lot, Local Dimming is hiding it. Set it one level lower (for example from High to Medium) and try again. Don’t keep raising Brightness: that makes every dark scene grey.

Uses the brightness test pattern. The guided setup shows it on your TV for you.

05

Contrast

This makes bright scenes look right, without losing detail in clouds, snow or white shirts.

Picture → Brightness → Contrast. Turn off Active Contrast as well.

When it’s right, you can see all three boxes. A is easy, B is lighter, C is very faint.

If box C never appears, also check that Dynamic contrast and any “HDR effect” or “Peak brightness” boost are off.

Uses the contrast test pattern. The guided setup shows it on your TV for you.

06

Sharpness

Most TVs add fake outlines to make the picture look crisper. Walk up close to the TV: lower Sharpness until the black lines have no bright outlines beside them.

Picture → Clarity → Sharpness.

Uses the sharpness test pattern. The guided setup shows it on your TV for you.

07

Colour temperature

Switch between the colour temperature options while watching the TV. The white and grey areas show the difference most clearly: cooler options look bluish, warmer ones look yellowish.

Picture → Color (or Advanced Settings) → Color Temperature. The options are usually Cool, Standard or Medium, Warm1 and Warm2.

Set Color Temperature to Warm2Picture → Color → Color Temperature. Flick to the cooler options to see the difference on the white and greys, then come back.

Give your eyes 30 seconds to adjust. Do the white and grey areas still look clearly yellow or orange?

If they still look yellow: try Warm1It’s one step cooler. Wait a moment and look again.

Uses the colour temperature test pattern. The guided setup shows it on your TV for you.

HDR optional

Movies and shows in HDR or Dolby Vision use separate picture settings on most TVs. This checks them in a few minutes.

Is HDR on?

Press the Info or Settings button while the pattern plays: the picture mode name usually shows “HDR” (for example HDR Filmmaker, HDR Movie or HDR Cinema).

Choose Filmmaker Mode or HDR Theater for HDRHisense keeps separate HDR picture settings. Check Active Contrast is off here too.

Not switching to HDR? If you’re casting to the TV’s own built-in Cast, HDR switches on by itself, so check the pattern is still playing. If you use a separate Chromecast in an HDMI port: set that port’s HDMI Format to Enhanced (Settings → Channels & Inputs → Inputs), and on the Chromecast set Dynamic range to match content.

HDR brightness

Same as before, but for HDR. Most TVs keep a separate Brightness setting for HDR.

Use Brightness while HDR is playing; changes usually only affect HDR. For HDR, Backlight is usually best at its maximum.

Highlights

Four bright boxes, from bright (A) to very bright (D). Most TVs can’t reach the brightest levels, so they compress them. This checks they do it gracefully.

Dynamic Tone Mapping is usually in Picture → Advanced Settings, and Peak Brightness in Picture → Brightness. Leave Contrast at the HDR mode’s default.

If some boxes look the same: turn on Dynamic Tone MappingUsually under Picture → Advanced Settings. If it’s already on, try Off. Then look at the boxes again.

Uses the HDR test patterns. The guided setup shows them on your TV for you.

Dolby Vision optional

Dolby Vision adjusts the picture to your TV scene by scene, so there’s no pattern to tune. A few settings still matter, and most TVs keep them separate from the HDR ones you just set.

Start a Dolby Vision film or show, then pause it on a normal sceneLook for the Dolby Vision label in Netflix, Disney+, Prime Video or Apple TV. The TV only shows its Dolby Vision settings while Dolby Vision is playing, and changes you make now only affect Dolby Vision.
Choose Dolby Vision Dark (or Filmmaker / Cinema if there’s no Dark)Dark is the most accurate. Bright and Vivid boost everything. IQ modes follow the room light, which is fine for daytime viewing in a bright room.
Turn off the extras and leave the rest at defaultTurn off dynamic contrast, colour enhancers, motion smoothing and energy saving here too. Leave Contrast, Colour and Gamma at this mode’s defaults: Dolby Vision is already fitting the picture to your TV.

Find a dark scene. Do black areas, like letterbox bars or a night sky, look grey or glowing?

If they look grey: press Brightness down by 1Small changes only. If dark details start to disappear, go back up by 1 and stop there.

Your devices and room optional

  • Your devices
  • HDMI black level: AutoFor anything plugged in by HDMI: set the device’s output range to Auto, and HDMI Dynamic Range on the TV to Auto. A mismatch crushes or washes out blacks.
  • Streaming device: match contentOn a separate Chromecast, Apple TV or Fire TV, turn on match dynamic range and match frame rate, so films play at their natural speed without judder.
  • Streaming device resolution: 4KIf a separate Chromecast or streaming box is plugged into a 4K TV, set its resolution to 4K or Auto (on a Chromecast with Google TV: Settings → Display & Sound → Resolution). At 1080p, the TV has to upscale everything you watch. TVs with Google TV or another smart system built in don’t need this.
  • Film motion: natural cadenceMotion Enhancement Off plays films as they were shot. If slow camera pans look jerky to you, try Motion Enhancement → Film.
  • Game console: game mode and HDR calibrationUse the Game picture mode on the console’s input, then run the console’s own HDR calibration screen.
  • Copy settings to other inputsRepeat the values from your summary on each input you use.
  • Streaming quality: HighIn Netflix, YouTube and other apps, set playback or data usage to High or Best.
  • Your room
  • Backlight: set for your roomOn most Hisense TVs, Backlight is the light output. Brighter for daytime, dimmer at night.
  • Reduce reflectionsClose blinds or move lamps that reflect in the screen. Glare lowers contrast more than any setting.
  • Bias lighting (optional)A dim, neutral-white light behind the TV (6500 K, about a tenth as bright as the screen) makes blacks look deeper and eases eye strain in a dark room.
  • SeatingEyes level with about the middle of the screen, sitting straight on. LCD colour and contrast fade when viewed from an angle.
