Brand guide

TCL picture setup

Using TCL Google TV menu names. Some older or Roku-based TCL TVs use different menus; if a name doesn’t match, look for the closest one.

Watching through a streaming box or console? Set that up first: a mismatch there makes blacks grey or crushed whatever you do on the TV.

01

Get ready

  • Update your TV’s software first: Settings → System → About → System update. Updates can change picture processing or reset settings, so do it before you start.
  • Turn the lights down to how you normally watch.
  • Open the picture menu with the remote: Settings → Display & Sound → Picture. You’ll use it throughout.
02

Quick settings

Do these first. They make the biggest difference.

  • Picture Mode: FILMMAKER MODE or MoviePicture → Picture Mode. Either is fine; FILMMAKER MODE also turns off most extra processing.
  • Motion Clarity: OffUnder Motion settings. LED Motion Clear (sharper motion, but can flicker) is a personal choice; leave it off if unsure.
  • Adaptive Brightness and energy saving: OffAdaptive Brightness is in the extra picture options (on some models under Eye Health Protection). Energy saving is under Settings → System → Power & Energy.
  • Color Space: AutoUnder Color settings. If there’s no Auto, leave it at the picture mode’s default.
  • Color Saturation and Tint: default; dynamic options OffTurn off Dynamic Color, Adaptive Color Temperature, Dynamic Contrast, Black Stretch and Dynamic Brightness.
  • Gamma: 2.4 in a dark room, 2.2 in a bright oneUnder Brightness settings. If those values aren’t offered, leave it at the default.
  • Noise Reduction and Digital Noise Reduction: OffUnder Clarity settings. Low is fine for an old, grainy source.
Where are these settings?

Settings → Display & Sound → Picture. The detailed controls are grouped as Brightness, Color, Clarity and Motion settings.

TCL keeps separate settings for HDR content, so the HDR steps later will check those.

03

Fit to screen

Look at the edges of your TV. There’s a thin white border around the very edge, with arrows in each corner.

Can you see the white border on all four edges, with arrows touching the corners?

If not: turn Overscan offDisplay & Sound → Screen settings → Overscan → Off. Also check Aspect Ratio is set to Auto.

Uses the fit-to-screen test pattern. The guided setup shows it on your TV for you.

04

Black Level

This makes dark scenes look right: true black, with shadow detail still visible. On TCL this is the Black Level setting.

On TCL, Brightness sets the backlight: how bright the whole screen is. For this step, use Black Level instead (Picture → Brightness settings → Black Level). Set Brightness itself to whatever is comfortable for your room.

Look inside the grey frames. When it’s right, box A is completely gone and box B is a very faint grey square.

Not changing? Make sure you’re adjusting Black Level, not Brightness. If box B never appears however high you go, see “About Local Contrast” below.

About Local Contrast (local dimming)

Many TCL TVs dim parts of the backlight in dark areas. TCL calls this Local Contrast (under Picture → Brightness settings).

The grey frames around the boxes keep the backlight on, like in a real dark scene. Judge the boxes inside the frames, not the black edges of the screen.

If box B still never appears, even after raising Black Level a lot, Local Contrast is hiding it. Set it one level lower (for example from High to Medium) and try again. Don’t keep raising Black Level: that makes every dark scene grey.

Uses the brightness test pattern. The guided setup shows it on your TV for you.

05

Contrast

This makes bright scenes look right, without losing detail in clouds, snow or white shirts.

Picture → Brightness settings → Contrast. Turn off Dynamic Contrast as well.

When it’s right, you can see all three boxes. A is easy, B is lighter, C is very faint.

If box C never appears, check that Dynamic Contrast is off and Peak Luminance is at its default.

Uses the contrast test pattern. The guided setup shows it on your TV for you.

06

Sharpness

Most TVs add fake outlines to make the picture look crisper. Walk up close to the TV: lower Sharpness until the black lines have no bright outlines beside them.

Picture → Clarity settings → Sharpness. If your model has Super Resolution or Precision Detail, turn them off too.

Uses the sharpness test pattern. The guided setup shows it on your TV for you.

07

Colour temperature

Switch between the colour temperature options while watching the TV. The white and grey areas show the difference most clearly: cooler options look bluish, warmer ones look yellowish.

Picture → Color settings → Color Temperature. The options are usually Cool, Normal and one or more Warm settings; some models offer several Warm levels. Turn off Adaptive Color Temperature too.

Set Color Temperature to its warmest Warm optionPicture → Color settings → Color Temperature. Flick to Normal and Cool to see the difference on the white and greys, then come back.

Give your eyes 30 seconds to adjust. Do the white and grey areas still look clearly yellow or orange?

If they still look yellow: go one step toward NormalFor example the next Warm level down. Then wait a moment and look again.

Uses the colour temperature test pattern. The guided setup shows it on your TV for you.

HDR optional

Movies and shows in HDR or Dolby Vision use separate picture settings on most TVs. This checks them in a few minutes.

Is HDR on?

While the HDR pattern plays, open Settings → Display & Sound → Picture: the picture mode list switches to its HDR versions.

Choose FILMMAKER MODE or Movie for HDRTCL keeps a separate picture mode for HDR. Turn off Dynamic Contrast and Adaptive Brightness here too.

Not switching to HDR? If you’re casting to the TV’s built-in Google TV, HDR switches on by itself, so check the pattern is still playing. If you use a separate Chromecast in an HDMI port: set that port’s HDMI Mode to HDMI 2.1 or Auto in the TV’s input settings, and on the Chromecast set Dynamic range to match content.

HDR Black Level

Same as before, but for HDR. Most TVs keep a separate Brightness setting for HDR.

Use Black Level (Picture → Brightness settings) while HDR is playing. For HDR, set Brightness (the backlight) high, since HDR needs it.

Highlights

Four bright boxes, from bright (A) to very bright (D). Most TVs can’t reach the brightest levels, so they compress them. This checks they do it gracefully.

Dynamic Tone Mapping and Peak Luminance are under Picture → Brightness settings. Leave Contrast at the HDR mode’s default.

If some boxes look the same: turn on Dynamic Tone MappingPicture → Brightness settings → Dynamic Tone Mapping. If it’s already on, try it off. Then look at the boxes again.

Uses the HDR test patterns. The guided setup shows them on your TV for you.

Dolby Vision optional

Dolby Vision adjusts the picture to your TV scene by scene, so there’s no pattern to tune. A few settings still matter, and most TVs keep them separate from the HDR ones you just set.

Start a Dolby Vision film or show, then pause it on a normal sceneLook for the Dolby Vision label in Netflix, Disney+, Prime Video or Apple TV. The TV only shows its Dolby Vision settings while Dolby Vision is playing, and changes you make now only affect Dolby Vision.
Choose Dolby Vision Dark (or Filmmaker / Cinema if there’s no Dark)Dark is the most accurate. Bright and Vivid boost everything. IQ modes follow the room light, which is fine for daytime viewing in a bright room.
Turn off the extras and leave the rest at defaultTurn off dynamic contrast, colour enhancers, motion smoothing and energy saving here too. Leave Contrast, Colour and Gamma at this mode’s defaults: Dolby Vision is already fitting the picture to your TV.

Find a dark scene. Do black areas, like letterbox bars or a night sky, look grey or glowing?

If they look grey: press Black Level down by 1Not Brightness, which is the backlight on TCL. Small changes only; if dark details start to disappear, go back up by 1 and stop there.

Your devices and room optional

  • Your devices
  • HDMI black level: AutoFor anything plugged in by HDMI (game console, Blu-ray, streaming stick). Set the device’s output range to Auto, and leave the TV’s matching setting at Auto if it has one. A mismatch crushes or washes out blacks.
  • Streaming device: match contentOn a separate Chromecast, Apple TV or Fire TV, turn on match dynamic range and match frame rate, so films play at their natural speed without judder.
  • Streaming device resolution: 4KIf a separate Chromecast or streaming box is plugged into a 4K TV, set its resolution to 4K or Auto (on a Chromecast with Google TV: Settings → Display & Sound → Resolution). At 1080p, the TV has to upscale everything you watch. TVs with Google TV or another smart system built in don’t need this.
  • Film motion: natural cadenceMotion Clarity Off plays films as they were shot. If slow camera pans look jerky to you, try Motion Clarity → Custom with a little Judder Reduction.
  • Game console: game mode and HDR calibrationUse the TV’s game picture mode (Game Master on some TCL models) on the console’s input, then run the console’s own HDR calibration screen.
  • Copy settings to other inputsIn the picture options, use “Apply All Picture Settings” to copy them, or repeat the values from your summary on each input you use.
  • Streaming quality: HighIn Netflix, YouTube and other apps, set playback or data usage to High or Best.
  • Your room
  • Brightness: set for your roomOn TCL, Brightness is the backlight. Brighter for daytime, dimmer at night.
  • Reduce reflectionsClose blinds or move lamps that reflect in the screen. Glare lowers contrast more than any setting.
  • Bias lighting (optional)A dim, neutral-white light behind the TV (6500 K, about a tenth as bright as the screen) makes blacks look deeper and eases eye strain in a dark room.
  • SeatingEyes level with about the middle of the screen, sitting straight on. LCD colour and contrast fade when viewed from an angle.
