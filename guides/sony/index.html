Brand guide

Sony picture setup

Using Sony BRAVIA Google TV menu names. Older Android TV models are similar but some names differ; look for the closest one.

Watching through a streaming box or console? Set that up first: a mismatch there makes blacks grey or crushed whatever you do on the TV.

01

Get ready

  • Update your TV’s software first: Settings → System → About → System software update. Updates can change picture processing or reset settings, so do it before you start.
  • Turn the lights down to how you normally watch.
  • Open the picture menu with the remote: Settings → Display & Sound → Picture. You’ll use it throughout.
02

Quick settings

Do these first. They make the biggest difference.

  • Picture mode: Professional, Custom or CinemaDisplay & Sound → Picture → Picture mode. Professional (on newer models) and Custom are the most accurate.
  • Motionflow: OffPicture → Motion. Leave CineMotion on Auto: it helps films play smoothly.
  • Light sensor and power saving: OffLight sensor is in the picture settings (with Auto luminance level on some models). Turn off power saving in the power or eco settings.
  • Color space: AutoMost Sony picture modes set this automatically. If you see a Color space option, choose Auto.
  • Color and Hue: default; Live Color OffAlso turn off Adv. contrast enhancer and Black adjust.
  • Gamma: leave at the defaultSony uses numbers (−3 to +3) instead of 2.2 or 2.4. If shadows look too dark in a bright room, try one step lower.
  • Noise reduction: OffPicture → Clarity: turn off Random noise reduction and Digital noise reduction. Turn Reality Creation off too.
Where are these settings?

Settings → Display & Sound → Picture, grouped into Brightness, Color, Clarity and Motion.

Sony keeps separate settings for HDR content, so the HDR steps later will check those.

03

Fit to screen

Look at the edges of your TV. There’s a thin white border around the very edge, with arrows in each corner.

Can you see the white border on all four edges, with arrows touching the corners?

If not: set Display area to Full pixelLook in Settings → Display & Sound → Screen for Display area → Full pixel. If you see Wide mode, set it to Full.

Uses the fit-to-screen test pattern. The guided setup shows it on your TV for you.

04

Black level

This makes dark scenes look right: true black, with shadow detail still visible. On Sony this is the Black level setting.

Picture → Brightness → Black level (the default is 50). On Sony, Brightness sets the screen’s light output: set it for comfort.

Look inside the grey frames. When it’s right, box A is completely gone and box B is a very faint grey square.

Not changing? Make sure you’re adjusting Black level, not Brightness. If box B never appears, see “About Auto local dimming” below.

About Auto local dimming

Sony OLED TVs don’t have this, so it only applies to LCD models.

Many LCD TVs dim parts of the backlight in dark areas. On this TV it’s called Auto local dimming.

The grey frames around the boxes keep the backlight on, like in a real dark scene. Judge the boxes inside the frames, not the black edges of the screen.

If box B still never appears, even after raising Black level a lot, Auto local dimming is hiding it. Set it one level lower (for example from High to Medium) and try again. Don’t keep raising Black level: that makes every dark scene grey.

Uses the brightness test pattern. The guided setup shows it on your TV for you.

05

Contrast

This makes bright scenes look right, without losing detail in clouds, snow or white shirts.

Picture → Brightness → Contrast. Turn off Adv. contrast enhancer as well.

When it’s right, you can see all three boxes. A is easy, B is lighter, C is very faint.

If box C never appears, also check that Dynamic contrast and any “HDR effect” or “Peak brightness” boost are off.

Uses the contrast test pattern. The guided setup shows it on your TV for you.

06

Sharpness

Most TVs add fake outlines to make the picture look crisper. Walk up close to the TV: lower Sharpness until the black lines have no bright outlines beside them.

Picture → Clarity → Sharpness. On Sony, 50 is neutral: going below it softens the picture. Turn off Reality Creation too.

But don’t go below 50, which is neutral on Sony. Then look closely at the lines again.

Uses the sharpness test pattern. The guided setup shows it on your TV for you.

07

Colour temperature

Switch between the colour temperature options while watching the TV. The white and grey areas show the difference most clearly: cooler options look bluish, warmer ones look yellowish.

Picture → Color → Color temperature. The options are Cool, Natural, Warm, Expert1 and Expert2. Expert1 is the one designed to match the film standard.

Set Color temperature to Expert1Picture → Color → Color temperature. Flick to Natural and Cool to see the difference on the white and greys, then come back.

Give your eyes 30 seconds to adjust. Do the white and grey areas still look clearly yellow or orange?

If they still look yellow: try Expert2Then wait a moment and look again. If it still looks yellow, try Natural.

Uses the colour temperature test pattern. The guided setup shows it on your TV for you.

HDR optional

Movies and shows in HDR or Dolby Vision use separate picture settings on most TVs. This checks them in a few minutes.

Is HDR on?

Press the Info or Settings button while the pattern plays: the picture mode name usually shows “HDR” (for example HDR Filmmaker, HDR Movie or HDR Cinema).

Choose Professional, Custom or Cinema for HDRSony keeps separate HDR picture settings. Check Adv. contrast enhancer and Live Color are off here too.

Not switching to HDR? If you’re casting to the TV’s own built-in Cast, HDR switches on by itself, so check the pattern is still playing. If you use a separate Chromecast in an HDMI port: set that port’s HDMI signal format to Enhanced format (Settings → Channels & Inputs → External inputs), and on the Chromecast set Dynamic range to match content.

HDR Black level

Same as before, but for HDR. Most TVs keep a separate Brightness setting for HDR.

Use Black level while HDR is playing; changes usually only affect HDR. For HDR, Brightness (the light output) is usually best at its maximum.

Highlights

Four bright boxes, from bright (A) to very bright (D). Most TVs can’t reach the brightest levels, so they compress them. This checks they do it gracefully.

HDR tone mapping and Peak luminance are in Picture → Brightness (not on every model). Leave Contrast at the HDR mode’s default.

If some boxes look the same: change HDR tone mappingPicture → Brightness → HDR tone mapping. Try its other options, then look at the boxes again.

Uses the HDR test patterns. The guided setup shows them on your TV for you.

Dolby Vision optional

Dolby Vision adjusts the picture to your TV scene by scene, so there’s no pattern to tune. A few settings still matter, and most TVs keep them separate from the HDR ones you just set.

Start a Dolby Vision film or show, then pause it on a normal sceneLook for the Dolby Vision label in Netflix, Disney+, Prime Video or Apple TV. The TV only shows its Dolby Vision settings while Dolby Vision is playing, and changes you make now only affect Dolby Vision.
Choose Dolby Vision Dark (or Filmmaker / Cinema if there’s no Dark)Dark is the most accurate. Bright and Vivid boost everything. IQ modes follow the room light, which is fine for daytime viewing in a bright room.
Turn off the extras and leave the rest at defaultTurn off dynamic contrast, colour enhancers, motion smoothing and energy saving here too. Leave Contrast, Colour and Gamma at this mode’s defaults: Dolby Vision is already fitting the picture to your TV.

Find a dark scene. Do black areas, like letterbox bars or a night sky, look grey or glowing?

If they look grey: press Black level down by 1Small changes only. If dark details start to disappear, go back up by 1 and stop there.

Your devices and room optional

  • Your devices
  • HDMI black level: AutoFor anything plugged in by HDMI: set the device’s output range to Auto, and HDMI video range on the TV (Picture → Video options) to Auto. A mismatch crushes or washes out blacks.
  • Streaming device: match contentOn a separate Chromecast, Apple TV or Fire TV, turn on match dynamic range and match frame rate, so films play at their natural speed without judder.
  • Streaming device resolution: 4KIf a separate Chromecast or streaming box is plugged into a 4K TV, set its resolution to 4K or Auto (on a Chromecast with Google TV: Settings → Display & Sound → Resolution). At 1080p, the TV has to upscale everything you watch. TVs with Google TV or another smart system built in don’t need this.
  • Film motion: natural cadenceMotionflow Off with CineMotion on Auto plays films smoothly at their natural cadence.
  • Game console: game mode and HDR calibrationUse the Game picture mode on the console’s input, then run the console’s own HDR calibration screen.
  • Copy settings to other inputsRepeat the values from your summary on each input you use; Sony keeps picture settings per input.
  • Streaming quality: HighIn Netflix, YouTube and other apps, set playback or data usage to High or Best.
  • Your room
  • Brightness: set for your roomOn Sony, Brightness is the light output. Brighter for daytime, dimmer at night.
  • Reduce reflectionsClose blinds or move lamps that reflect in the screen. Glare lowers contrast more than any setting.
  • Bias lighting (optional)A dim, neutral-white light behind the TV (6500 K, about a tenth as bright as the screen) makes blacks look deeper and eases eye strain in a dark room.
  • SeatingEyes level with about the middle of the screen, sitting straight on. LCD colour and contrast fade when viewed from an angle.
