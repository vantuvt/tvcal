Brand guide

Samsung picture setup

Using Samsung menu names (2022 and later, with notes for older models). If a name doesn’t match, look for the closest one.

Watching through a streaming box or console? Set that up first: a mismatch there makes blacks grey or crushed whatever you do on the TV.

01

Get ready

  • Update your TV’s software first: Settings → All Settings → Support → Software Update → Update Now. Updates can change picture processing or reset settings, so do it before you start.
  • Turn the lights down to how you normally watch.
  • Open the picture menu with the remote: Settings → All Settings → Picture. You’ll use it throughout.
02

Quick settings

Do these first. They make the biggest difference.

  • Picture Mode: Filmmaker Mode or MoviePicture → Picture Mode.
  • Picture Clarity: OffPicture → Expert Settings → Picture Clarity Settings (Auto Motion Plus on older models). LED Clear Motion is a personal choice: sharper motion, but a dimmer picture. Leave it off if unsure.
  • Brightness Optimization and energy saving: OffSettings → All Settings → General & Privacy → Power and Energy Saving.
  • Colour Space Settings: AutoPicture → Expert Settings → Colour Space Settings. Avoid Native.
  • Colour and Tint: default; Colour Booster OffAlso turn off Contrast Enhancer and Auto HDR Remastering if your model has them.
  • Gamma: BT.1886 in a dark room, 2.2 in a bright onePicture → Expert Settings → Gamma. BT.1886 is similar to 2.4.
  • Noise reduction: OffLook for Noise Reduction in Picture Clarity Settings, or Digital Clean View on older models.
Where are these settings?

Settings → All Settings → Picture → Expert Settings (on 2021 and older models: Settings → Picture → Expert Settings).

Samsung keeps separate settings for HDR content, so the HDR steps later will check those.

03

Fit to screen

Look at the edges of your TV. There’s a thin white border around the very edge, with arrows in each corner.

Can you see the white border on all four edges, with arrows touching the corners?

If not: turn on Fit to ScreenPicture → Picture Size Settings: set Picture Size to 16:9 Standard and turn on Fit to Screen.

Uses the fit-to-screen test pattern. The guided setup shows it on your TV for you.

04

Shadow Detail

This makes dark scenes look right: true black, with shadow detail still visible. On Samsung TVs from 2022 on, this is Shadow Detail. On older models, use Brightness.

Picture → Expert Settings → Shadow Detail. On these models, Brightness sets the overall light output: set it for comfort.

On 2021 and older models there’s a separate Backlight for light output, and Brightness is the control for this step.

Look inside the grey frames. When it’s right, box A is completely gone and box B is a very faint grey square.

Not changing? On 2022 and later models use Shadow Detail (Brightness only changes the light output). On older models use Brightness. If box B never appears, see “About Local Dimming” below.

About Local Dimming

Samsung OLED TVs don’t have this, so it only applies to LCD (QLED and Neo QLED) models.

Many LCD TVs dim parts of the backlight in dark areas. On this TV it’s called Local Dimming.

The grey frames around the boxes keep the backlight on, like in a real dark scene. Judge the boxes inside the frames, not the black edges of the screen.

If box B still never appears, even after raising Shadow Detail a lot, Local Dimming is hiding it. Set it one level lower (for example from High to Medium) and try again. Don’t keep raising Shadow Detail: that makes every dark scene grey.

Uses the brightness test pattern. The guided setup shows it on your TV for you.

05

Contrast

This makes bright scenes look right, without losing detail in clouds, snow or white shirts.

Picture → Expert Settings → Contrast. Turn off Contrast Enhancer as well.

When it’s right, you can see all three boxes. A is easy, B is lighter, C is very faint.

If box C never appears, also check that Dynamic contrast and any “HDR effect” or “Peak brightness” boost are off.

Uses the contrast test pattern. The guided setup shows it on your TV for you.

06

Sharpness

Most TVs add fake outlines to make the picture look crisper. Walk up close to the TV: lower Sharpness until the black lines have no bright outlines beside them.

Picture → Expert Settings → Sharpness. 0 adds no artificial sharpening on most models.

Uses the sharpness test pattern. The guided setup shows it on your TV for you.

07

Colour temperature

Switch between the colour temperature options while watching the TV. The white and grey areas show the difference most clearly: cooler options look bluish, warmer ones look yellowish.

Picture → Expert Settings → Colour Tone. The options are Cool, Standard, Warm1 and Warm2.

Set Colour Tone to Warm2Picture → Expert Settings → Colour Tone. Flick to Standard and Cool to see the difference on the white and greys, then come back.

Give your eyes 30 seconds to adjust. Do the white and grey areas still look clearly yellow or orange?

If they still look yellow: try Warm1It’s one step cooler. Wait a moment and look again.

Uses the colour temperature test pattern. The guided setup shows it on your TV for you.

HDR optional

Movies and shows in HDR or Dolby Vision use separate picture settings on most TVs. This checks them in a few minutes.

Is HDR on?

Press the Info or Settings button while the pattern plays: the picture mode name usually shows “HDR” (for example HDR Filmmaker, HDR Movie or HDR Cinema).

Choose Filmmaker Mode or Movie for HDRSamsung keeps separate HDR picture settings. Check Contrast Enhancer is off here too.

Not switching to HDR? If you’re casting to the TV’s own built-in Cast, HDR switches on by itself, so check the pattern is still playing. If you use a separate Chromecast in an HDMI port: turn on Input Signal Plus for that port (Settings → All Settings → Connection → External Device Manager), and on the Chromecast set Dynamic range to match content.

HDR Shadow Detail

Same as before, but for HDR. Most TVs keep a separate Brightness setting for HDR.

Use Shadow Detail while HDR is playing; changes usually only affect HDR. For HDR, set Brightness (the light output) to its maximum.

Highlights

Four bright boxes, from bright (A) to very bright (D). Most TVs can’t reach the brightest levels, so they compress them. This checks they do it gracefully.

HDR Tone Mapping and Peak Brightness are in Picture → Expert Settings (not on every model). Leave Contrast at the HDR mode’s default.

If some boxes look the same: change HDR Tone MappingPicture → Expert Settings → HDR Tone Mapping. Try the other option (Active or Static), then look at the boxes again.

Uses the HDR test patterns. The guided setup shows them on your TV for you.

Dolby Vision optional

Samsung TVs don’t use Dolby Vision.

Streaming apps send the same titles in HDR10 or HDR10+, which use the HDR settings you just set, so there’s nothing more to do here.

Your devices and room optional

  • Your devices
  • HDMI black level: AutoFor anything plugged in by HDMI: set the device’s output range to Auto, and HDMI Black Level on the TV to Auto if your model shows it (Expert Settings, HDMI inputs only). A mismatch crushes or washes out blacks.
  • Streaming device: match contentOn a separate Chromecast, Apple TV or Fire TV, turn on match dynamic range and match frame rate, so films play at their natural speed without judder.
  • Streaming device resolution: 4KIf a separate Chromecast or streaming box is plugged into a 4K TV, set its resolution to 4K or Auto (on a Chromecast with Google TV: Settings → Display & Sound → Resolution). At 1080p, the TV has to upscale everything you watch. TVs with Google TV or another smart system built in don’t need this.
  • Film motion: natural cadencePicture Clarity Off plays films as they were shot. If slow camera pans look jerky to you, try Picture Clarity → Custom with a little Judder Reduction.
  • Game console: game mode and HDR calibrationTurn on Game Mode for the console’s input (Settings → All Settings → Connection → Game Mode Settings), then run the console’s own HDR calibration screen.
  • Copy settings to other inputsPicture → Expert Settings → Apply Picture Settings → All Sources.
  • Streaming quality: HighIn Netflix, YouTube and other apps, set playback or data usage to High or Best.
  • Your room
  • Brightness: set for your roomOn Samsung (2022 and later), Brightness is the light output; on older models it’s Backlight. Brighter for daytime, dimmer at night.
  • Reduce reflectionsClose blinds or move lamps that reflect in the screen. Glare lowers contrast more than any setting.
  • Bias lighting (optional)A dim, neutral-white light behind the TV (6500 K, about a tenth as bright as the screen) makes blacks look deeper and eases eye strain in a dark room.
  • SeatingEyes level with about the middle of the screen, sitting straight on. LCD colour and contrast fade when viewed from an angle.
