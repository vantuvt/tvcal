Troubleshoot › Grey blacks

Blacks look grey or washed out

Dark scenes look foggy and night skies look dark grey instead of black. Work down the list: the most likely causes come first.

  1. Black level is set too high

    Most common

    The control that sets where black sits is often turned up to see more in dark scenes. Depending on the brand it’s called Brightness, Black Level, Shadow Detail or Screen Brightness.

    Run the guided setup

  2. Your box and TV disagree on range

    Video can be sent as limited range (black at 16) or full range (black at 0). If a streaming box, console or PC sends one and the TV expects the other, black arrives wrong before you touch a setting.

    Fix: set both the device and the TV to Auto, or set them to match. Then set black level again.

    Apple TV Google TV / Chromecast Fire TV PlayStation 5 Xbox PC

  3. The room is too bright

    Light from windows and lamps reflects off the screen, and no TV can look darker than its own reflection. Try the same scene with the lights down before changing anything.

  4. Local dimming is off or low

    On LCD TVs, local dimming turns the backlight down behind dark areas. With it off, the backlight glows through and blacks look grey. OLED TVs don’t have it and don’t need it.

    Turned up too high, it can hide shadow detail instead. Your brand guide explains what your TV calls it.

  5. It’s the panel, not a setting

    Some LCD panels can’t reach a deep black, especially seen from an angle. If black level is right and the room is dark, there may be nothing left to fix.
