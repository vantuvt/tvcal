Judder, 24p and the soap opera effect

Films are shot at 24 frames a second, and that slightly stepped motion is part of how a film looks. TVs can get it wrong in two opposite ways: by showing those frames unevenly, or by inventing new frames until film looks like video.

Why films judder on TV

Most TVs refresh 60 times a second, and 60 isn’t a multiple of 24. To fit, frames are held unevenly: one for three refreshes, the next for two, and so on. During slow camera pans that uneven rhythm shows up as a stutter, called judder.

The fix is to show every frame for the same time. A TV running at 120 Hz can hold each frame for exactly five refreshes, and some TVs switch to 24 Hz for films. Your streaming box needs to send films at 24 frames a second for this to work: that’s its match frame rate setting.

Even shown perfectly, 24 frames a second isn’t perfectly smooth. Fast pans look a little stepped in a cinema too. That’s the film look, and it’s how the director saw it.

The soap opera effect

Motion smoothing (also called motion interpolation) works out what the in-between frames would have looked like and adds them. Pans become silky smooth, but film loses its look and starts to resemble live TV or a home video. That’s the soap opera effect. Smoothing can also leave shimmering artefacts around moving edges.

Many directors have asked for it to be off by default. Filmmaker Mode turns it off, along with sharpening and noise reduction.

What to set

Films and drama
Motion smoothing off. If your TV has a natural-cinema option (Real Cinema, Cinematic Movement, CineMotion), turn it on for even 24p.
If pans bother you
Some TVs let you set a little judder reduction on its own, without blur reduction. A low setting smooths pans without the full video look. It’s a personal choice.
Sport
Usually broadcast at 50 or 60 frames a second already, so smoothing adds less. Some people like a low setting.
Games
Use Game mode. Smoothing adds delay between your controller and the screen.

OLED TVs switch pixels so fast that each film frame stays perfectly sharp, then jumps to the next. That can make 24p stutter more noticeable than on an LCD, and is why some OLED owners keep a little judder reduction.
